Beyond Memory, Toward Intelligence

I am currently a Research Fellow at the National University of Singapore, working in Prof. Xiao Gong's group. My research focuses on back-end-of-line (BEOL)-compatible oxide-semiconductor devices (e.g., IGZO, In2O3, and ZnO), monolithic 3D integration, high-density non-volatile memory, and compute-in-memory (CiM).

Current work includes oxide-semiconductor ferroelectric field-effect transistors (FeFETs), 3D ferroelectric vertical NAND (3D Fe-VNAND), 2T0C DRAM, and device reliability and variability studied through advanced electrical characterization, TCAD, and compact modeling. I have published 20 papers as first/co-first or corresponding/co-corresponding author, including 5 at the International Electron Devices Meeting (IEDM) and 3 at the VLSI Symposium, two of the premier international conferences in semiconductor devices and integrated-circuit technology. Overall, I have 46 journal and 56 conference publications, including 19 at IEDM and 26 at the VLSI Symposium.

Oxide-Semiconductor Devices & 3D Ferroelectric Memory

Oxide-semiconductor FeFETs provide a promising platform for dense, low-power memory that can be integrated above conventional CMOS circuits. A central challenge is achieving strong and reliable ferroelectric switching while maintaining favorable device electrostatics as the memory structure becomes more complex. Research in this direction combines oxide-channel engineering, MFMIS architectures, twin-gate designs, and ferroelectric-stack optimization to improve program and erase behavior. These device concepts are further extended from planar FeFETs toward vertically integrated 3D ferroelectric memory, enabling higher storage density while preserving compatibility with monolithic 3D integration.

Device Physics, Reliability & Modeling

Reliable ferroelectric memories depend on understanding how polarization switching, charge trapping, semiconductor transport, and structural variability interact during device operation. Electrical characterization is combined with physics-based analysis, TCAD, compact modeling, and stochastic simulation to separate these competing effects and connect microscopic mechanisms with measured electrical behavior. The resulting models are used to study memory-window formation, bias-stress degradation, soft breakdown, and device-to-device variability. Particular attention is given to how local transport paths, ferroelectric grain properties, and process variations influence switching behavior and ultimately affect the predictability of scaled memory devices and arrays.

Compute-in-Memory & Emerging Computing

Oxide-semiconductor and ferroelectric devices can serve not only as memory elements, but also as programmable building blocks for computation directly within or near memory. By exploiting analog charge storage, non-volatile polarization states, and device-level nonlinear dynamics, these technologies support a range of computing functions without relying on conventional data movement between processors and memory. Research in this direction spans neuromorphic computing, charge-domain processing, associative search, and electronic-photonic compute-in-memory. The broader goal is to connect emerging device functionality with practical array architectures and monolithic 3D systems for more energy-efficient memory-centric computing.
